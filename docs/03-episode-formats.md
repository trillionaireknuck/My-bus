# Episode Formats

Four formats. Everything you make should be one of them.

---

## Format 1 — The Song (2–4 min) · your core unit

This is the atom. You are not making videos, you are making songs that get
recompiled forever.

**Structure:**

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | **Benny's face, eye contact, already smiling.** No logo. No title card. |
| 0:03–0:10 | Hook — the first line of the chorus, sung, with the main visual gag |
| 0:10–2:30 | Verses. 4–6 verses, each a small variation on one idea |
| Last 15s | Wave goodbye + your end card / subscribe |

**Rules:**
- Chorus repeats between every verse. Always. Repetition is the product.
- One idea per verse. "The wipers go swish" is a verse. Do not put two ideas in one.
- Hold every shot 3–5 seconds. No fast cutting.
- Camera stays mostly locked. Slow push-ins only.
- End on the same shot you opened on — it makes the loop feel complete.

**The 3-second rule is the one that matters most.** Most new channels in this niche
open with a 10-second animated logo sting. That is where the audience leaves. Brand
at the end, never at the start.

---

## Format 2 — The Compilation (30–60 min) · your actual product

Stitch 10–20 songs into one long upload. This is what parents search for and what
earns the watch time.

- Put your **strongest song first** — it carries the retention for the whole video.
- Add **YouTube chapters** for every song (see the publishing checklist). The
  reference videos had 20 and 35 chapters. Chapters let a parent jump to a favourite,
  which lifts session time, and each chapter title is extra keyword surface.
- Re-cut the same songs into different compilations with different themes and
  thumbnails: "Bus Songs", "Colour Songs", "Bedtime", "Counting". One library, many
  products.
- Put the runtime in the title and on the thumbnail: **"40 min"**, **"1 Hour"**.

A song you make once should appear in five or more compilations over its life.

---

## Format 3 — Learning Segments · the reason parents allow it

Parents justify screen time with learning. Give them the justification explicitly.

Each of these is a song and a chapter:

- **Colours** — a differently coloured bus arrives each verse
- **Counting 1–10** — buses arrive one at a time and line up
- **Shapes** — wheels are circles, windows are squares, roof signs are triangles
- **Animals** — animal passengers board and make their sound
- **Vehicles** — "Different Types of Buses": school bus, double-decker, minibus, tour bus
- **Routines** — bath time, bedtime, brushing teeth, tidying up

The vehicles and counting formats are the two that historically perform hardest in
this niche, which is exactly what the two videos in your screenshot are.

---

## Format 4 — Shorts (9:16, under 60s) · discovery only

Cut the single best 20–40 seconds out of a song, reframed vertical.

- Shorts do **not** monetise well on kids content and do not build watch time.
- They are for discovery and for the algorithm to learn who your audience is.
- Post them, but never at the expense of the long-form catalogue.
- Reframe to 9:16 by moving the camera in on Benny's face — do not letterbox.

---

## A realistic production order

Do not try to build a 60-minute compilation first. Build in this order:

1. **Songs 1–5**: five separate 2–3 minute songs, using traditional melodies.
2. **First compilation**: those five, cut together with chapters, ~15 minutes.
3. **Songs 6–15**: keep going, one or two a week.
4. **Second compilation**: 30–40 minutes, better thumbnail, put the strongest first.
5. Repeat. Re-cut old songs into new themed compilations as you go.

The catalogue is the asset. Individual videos are not.

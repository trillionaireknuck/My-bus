# Who You Are Actually Making This For

The single most useful thing to understand about this niche: **the viewer is not the
customer.**

A 2-year-old does not search YouTube. A parent searches, hands over the phone, and
walks away — or autoplay / the YouTube Kids app serves the next video. That split
drives every production decision below:

- **The title and thumbnail sell to the adult.** ("40 minutes", "20 songs", "sleep")
- **The first 10 seconds and the pacing hold the toddler.**

Optimise those two things for two different people and you have the format. Optimise
both for the toddler and no one ever finds the video.

## Why the reference videos are long

Look at what ranked in the screenshot: 40 minutes, 1 hour 5 minutes. This is not an
accident and it is not padding.

Parents deliberately search for *long* videos — car journeys, dinner prep, a flight.
A brilliant 2-minute song loses to a mediocre 45-minute compilation because the
parent is solving "occupy my child for the length of a car ride."

**So the compilation is the product. Individual songs are raw material.**

Practical consequence for a small channel: build a library of 2–4 minute songs, then
re-cut them into 30–60 minute compilations. One song = one asset you will reuse
across many long uploads for years. Never make a song you can only use once.

## Why the melodies are all traditional

"Wheels on the Bus", "Old MacDonald", "Twinkle Twinkle" are traditional/public-domain
melodies. That is exactly why every big channel uses them — they carry enormous,
permanent search volume that an original song simply does not have.

- **Public-domain melody = free discovery.** People search the song name.
- **Original songs = zero search volume**, but they build your brand.

Lead with traditional melodies to get found; add originals once people arrive.

> **Important:** the *melody* being public domain does not make someone else's
> *recording or arrangement* free to use. Record and arrange your own version. Also
> do not reproduce another channel's character designs — the red double-decker and
> the specific bus characters in that screenshot are their intellectual property.
> A cute stylised bus is a genre. Their bus is not.

## What actually holds a 1–5 year old

This age band is not one audience. Roughly:

| Age | What holds attention | What loses them |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Faces, eye contact, repetition, single moving object | Fast cuts, plot, dialogue |
| 2–3 | Simple cause-and-effect, counting, colours, naming | Abstract ideas, irony |
| 3–5 | Tiny stories, problem/solution, anticipation | Anything slow or wordless for long |

Design for the middle and the edges still work.

The visual rules that matter most:

1. **Show a face with eye contact in the first 3 seconds.** Infants track eyes from
   birth. Do not open on a logo animation — that is the most common fatal mistake in
   this niche. Brand at the *end*, not the start.
2. **High saturation and high contrast.** A developing visual system resolves bold,
   saturated colour far more easily than soft pastel. This is the main change your
   bus needed.
3. **One clear subject per frame.** Busy frames read as noise.
4. **Slow, predictable camera moves.** Under-2s cannot parse rapid cutting. Hold
   shots for 3–5 seconds.
5. **Repetition is a feature.** The same phrase four times is not lazy; it is how
   toddlers learn and it is why they re-watch.
6. **Smooth motion, no strobing or flashing.** Both a safety and a retention issue.

## Honest expectations

The videos in that screenshot have 2.5M and 6.1M views. They come from channels with
years of catalogue depth, hundreds or thousands of uploads, brand recognition, and
distribution inside the YouTube Kids app. Little Baby Bum was acquired by Moonbug;
these are funded operations.

A new channel does not match that with one video, and any advice that says otherwise
is selling something. What actually compounds in this niche:

- **Catalogue depth.** The algorithm rewards a deep, consistent library. Video 50
  performs better than video 1 partly *because* videos 1–49 exist.
- **Consistency of schedule and style.** Same character, same look, same slot.
- **Reusable assets.** Every song re-cuts into compilations forever.

Realistic shape: early videos in the hundreds of views, meaningful traction somewhere
in the first 30–100 uploads if the packaging is right, compounding after that. Plan
for a catalogue, not a hit.
